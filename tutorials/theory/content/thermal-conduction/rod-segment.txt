<p>
Now we combine the ideas of energy conservation and Fourier's law on a very small segment, which is termed a "control volume".
</p>

<p>
In addition to axial conduction, the segment can transfer heat radially with the surroundings. We call this radial transfer \(q_r\).
</p>

<p>
By convection:
</p>

<p>
$$
q_r = h P \, (T - T_\infty) dx
$$
</p>

<ul>
<li>\(h\) - convective heat transfer coefficient of the surrounding layer</li>
<li>\(P\) - perimeter of the rod</li>
<li>\(T_\infty\) - temperature of the surroundings</li>
<li>\(dx\) - length of the segment</li>
</ul>

<p>
Applying the energy balance to the segment at steady state (no accumulation, no internal generation):
</p>

<p>
$$
\text{Energy In} - \text{Energy Out} - \text{Radial Transfer} = 0
$$
</p>

<p>
Substituting the expressions for each term:
</p>

<p>
$$
q_x - q_{x+dx} - q_r = 0
$$
</p>

<p>
Dividing by \(dx\) and taking the limit as \(dx \to 0\), the difference \(q_{x+dx}-q_x\) becomes a derivative:
</p>

<p>
$$
\frac{dq_x}{dx} - \frac{q_r}{dx} = 0
$$
</p>

<p>
Substituting Fourier's law \(q_x=-kA \frac{dT}{dx}\):
</p>

<p>
$$
\frac{d}{dx}\Big[-kA \frac{dT}{dx}\Big] - \frac{q_r}{dx} = 0
$$
</p>

<!-- Force new page on print -->
<div class="print-page-break"></div>

<p>
Simplifying, assuming \(k\) and \(A\) are constant along the rod:
</p>

<p>
$$
kA \frac{d^2T}{dx^2} - \frac{q_r}{dx} = 0
$$
</p>

<p>
This is the governing differential equation describing the temperature distribution along a rod exchanging heat with its surroundings. The terms represent:
</p>

<ul>
<li>\(kA \frac{d^2T}{dx^2}\) - axial conduction into and out of the segment</li>
<li>\(\frac{q_r}{dx}\) - radial heat transfer with the surroundings \(h P \, (T - T_\infty)\)</li>
</ul>

</div>

<p>
It's convenient to express temperature relative to the surroundings by defining:
</p>

<p>
$$
\theta = T - T_\infty
$$
</p>

<p>
Substituting this gives:
</p>

<p>
$$
\frac{d^2 \theta}{dx^2} - \frac{hP}{kA} \theta = 0
$$
</p>

<p>
The grouped term
</p>

<p>
$$
m^2 = \frac{hP}{kA}
$$
</p>

<p>
is known as the square of the fin parameter. Using this definition, the governing equation takes the compact form:
</p>

<p>
$$
\frac{d^2 \theta}{dx^2} - m^2 \theta = 0
$$
</p>

<!-- Force new page on print -->
<div class="print-page-break"></div>

<p>
The fin parameter \(m\) characterises how strongly the rod exchanges heat with its surroundings relative to how effectively heat is conducted along its length.
</p>

<ul>
<li>\(h\) - strength of heat transfer between the rod surface and the surrounding fluid</li>
<li>\(P\) - perimeter of the rod influencing the heat exchange area</li>
<li>\(k\) - how effectively heat can conduct along the rod material</li>
<li>\(A\) - cross-sectional area for axial conduction</li>
</ul>

<p>
Physically, this equation describes the balance between axial conduction within the rod and heat exchange with the environment along its surface.
</p>

<ul>
<li>If \(m\) is small, conduction along the rod dominates and temperature changes slowly with distance</li>
<li>If \(m\) is large, heat exchange with the surroundings dominates and the temperature approaches the surrounding temperature rapidly</li>
</ul>
