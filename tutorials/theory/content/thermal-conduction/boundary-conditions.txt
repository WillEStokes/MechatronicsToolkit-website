<p>
To determine the temperature distribution along the rod we apply boundary conditions to the general solution of the governing differential equation:
</p>

<p>
$$
\theta(x) = A \cosh(mx) + B \sinh(mx)
$$
</p>

<h3>1. Thermally Constant Base and Convective Heat Transfer at the Tip</h3>

<h4>Base of the Rod (x = 0)</h4>

<div class="image-centre-shrink">
    <img src="./content/thermal-conduction/boundary-conditions.png">
</div>

<p>
The rod is thermally anchored at the base, so its temperature difference relative to the surroundings is:
</p>

<p>
$$
\theta(0) = \theta_b
$$
</p>

<p>
Substituting \(x = 0\) into the general solution:
</p>

<p>
$$
\theta_b = A \cosh(0) + B \sinh(0)
$$
</p>

<p>
Using the identities:
</p>

<p>
$$
\cosh(0)=1, \qquad \sinh(0)=0
$$
</p>

<p>
we obtain:
</p>

<p>
$$
\theta_b = A
$$
</p>

<p>
So the temperature distribution becomes:
</p>

<p>
$$
\theta(x) = \theta_b \cosh(mx) + B \sinh(mx)
$$
</p>

<!-- Force new page on print -->
<div class="print-page-break"></div>

<h4>Tip of the Rod (x = L)</h4>

<p>
At the tip of the rod, heat arriving by conduction along the rod must equal the heat leaving the tip.  
This heat loss can occur either by convection or conduction.
</p>

<p>
Heat arriving at the tip by conduction along the rod is given by Fourier’s law:
</p>

<p>
$$
q_x\big|_{x=L} = -k_f A_k \left(\frac{dT}{dx}\right)_{x=L}
$$
</p>

<ul>
<li>\(k_f\) - thermal conductivity of the rod</li>
<li>\(A_k\) - cross-sectional area of the rod</li>
</ul>

<h4>Convective Heat Loss from the Tip</h4>

<p>
If the tip is exposed to a surrounding fluid, heat leaves the tip by convection:
</p>

<p>
$$
q_e = h_e A_k (T_L - T_\infty)
$$
</p>

<p>
At steady state, heat conducted to the tip equals heat leaving the tip:
</p>

<p>
$$
-k_f A_k \left(\frac{dT}{dx}\right)_{x=L}
=
h_e A_k (T_L - T_\infty)
$$
</p>

<p>
Dividing by \(k_f A_k\):
</p>

<p>
$$
-\left(\frac{dT}{dx}\right)_{x=L}
=
\frac{h_e}{k_f}(T_L - T_\infty)
$$
</p>

<p>
Since \( \theta = T - T_\infty \), this becomes:
</p>

<p>
$$
-\left(\frac{d\theta}{dx}\right)_{x=L} = \frac{h_e}{k_f}\theta(L)
$$
</p>

<p>
or equivalently:
</p>

<p>
$$
\frac{d\theta}{dx}\Big|_{x=L} + \frac{h_e}{k_f}\theta(L) = 0
$$
</p>

<h4>Applying the Boundary Condition to the Hyperbolic Solution</h4>

<p>
The general solution of the governing differential equation is
</p>

<p>
$$
\theta(x) = A\cosh(mx) + B\sinh(mx)
$$
</p>

<p>
Its derivative is
</p>

<p>
$$
\frac{d\theta}{dx}
=
A m\sinh(mx)
+
B m\cosh(mx)
$$
</p>

<p>
Plugging these into the equation \(\frac{d\theta}{dx}\Big|_{x=L} + \frac{h_e}{k_f}\theta(L) = 0\) for the boundary condition at \(x=L\):
</p>

<p>
$$
A m \sinh(mL)
+
B m \cosh(mL)
+
\frac{h}{k}
\big(A\cosh(mL)+B\sinh(mL)\big)
=
0
$$
</p>

<h4>Solving for \(B\)</h4>

<p>
Expand the expression:
</p>

<p>
$$
A m\sinh(mL)
+
B m\cosh(mL)
+
\frac{h}{k}A\cosh(mL)
+
\frac{h}{k}B\sinh(mL)
=
0
$$
</p>

<p>
Group the constants:
</p>

<p>
$$
A\left[m\sinh(mL)+\frac{h}{k}\cosh(mL)\right]
+
B\left[m\cosh(mL)+\frac{h}{k}\sinh(mL)\right]
=
0
$$
</p>

<p>
Solving for \(B\):
</p>

<p>
$$
B
=
-
A
\frac{m\sinh(mL)+\frac{h}{k}\cosh(mL)}
{m\cosh(mL)+\frac{h}{k}\sinh(mL)}
$$
</p>

<p>
Using the base boundary condition \(A=\theta_b\):
</p>

<p>
$$
B
=
-
\theta_b
\frac{m\sinh(mL)+\frac{h}{k}\cosh(mL)}
{m\cosh(mL)+\frac{h}{k}\sinh(mL)}
$$
</p>

<!-- Force new page on print -->
<div class="print-page-break"></div>

<h4>Substituting Back into the Solution</h4>

<p>
$$
\theta(x)
=
\theta_b\cosh(mx)
-
\theta_b
\frac{m\sinh(mL)+\frac{h}{k}\cosh(mL)}
{m\cosh(mL)+\frac{h}{k}\sinh(mL)}
\sinh(mx)
$$
</p>

<p>
Dividing through by \( \theta_b \) gives the normalised temperature distribution:
</p>

<p>
$$
\frac{\theta(x)}{\theta_b}
=
\cosh(mx)
-
\frac{m\sinh(mL)+\frac{h}{k}\cosh(mL)}
{m\cosh(mL)+\frac{h}{k}\sinh(mL)}
\sinh(mx)
$$
</p>

<p>
Using hyperbolic identities and rearranging terms, this expression can be written in a more compact form:
</p>

<p>
$$
\frac{\theta(x)}{\theta_b}
=
\frac{
\cosh\big(m(L-x)\big)
+
\frac{h}{mk}\sinh\big(m(L-x)\big)
}{
\cosh(mL)
+
\frac{h}{mk}\sinh(mL)
}
$$
</p>

<ul>
<li>\(\theta_b\) - temperature difference at the base relative to the surroundings</li>
<li>\(\cosh(m(L-x))\) - describes the conductive temperature decay along the rod</li>
<li>\(\sinh(m(L-x))\) - accounts for the effect of heat loss from the tip</li>
<li>\(\frac{h}{mk}\) - dimensionless parameter representing the relative strength of tip convection</li>
</ul>

<h3>2. No Heat Transfer: Adiabatic (Insulated) Tip</h3>

<p>
If the tip is insulated, no heat leaves the end of the rod:
</p>

<p>
$$
q_x\big|_{x=L} = 0
$$
</p>

<p>
From Fourier’s law this means the temperature gradient at the tip must be zero:
</p>

<p>
$$
\left(\frac{dT}{dx}\right)_{x=L} = 0
$$
</p>

<p>
Since \( \theta = T - T_\infty \), the same condition applies to \( \theta \):
</p>

<p>
$$
\left(\frac{d\theta}{dx}\right)_{x=L} = 0
$$
</p>

<h4>Applying the Boundary Condition to the Hyperbolic Solution</h4>

<p>
The general solution for the temperature distribution along the rod is:
</p>

<p>
$$
\theta(x) = A\cosh(mx) + B\sinh(mx)
$$
</p>

<p>
The derivative of this expression is:
</p>

<p>
$$
\frac{d\theta}{dx}
=
A m\sinh(mx)
+
B m\cosh(mx)
$$
</p>

<p>
Plugging these into the equation \(\frac{d\theta}{dx}\Big|_{x=L} = 0\) for the adiabatic boundary condition at \(x=L\):
</p>

<p>
$$
A m\sinh(mL)
+
B m\cosh(mL)
=
0
$$
</p>

<h4>Solving for \(B\)</h4>

<p>
Divide through by \(m\):
</p>

<p>
$$
A\sinh(mL)
+
B\cosh(mL)
=
0
$$
</p>

<p>
Rearranging gives:
</p>

<p>
$$
B\cosh(mL) = -A\sinh(mL)
$$
</p>

<p>
so
</p>

<p>
$$
B = -A \tanh(mL)
$$
</p>

<p>
Using the base boundary condition \(A = \theta_b\):
</p>

<p>
$$
B = -\theta_b \tanh(mL)
$$
</p>

<!-- Force new page on print -->
<div class="print-page-break"></div>

<h4>Substituting Back into the Solution</h4>

<p>
Substituting \(A\) and \(B\) into the general solution:
</p>

<p>
$$
\theta(x)
=
\theta_b\cosh(mx)
-
\theta_b\tanh(mL)\sinh(mx)
$$
</p>

<p>
Dividing through by \( \theta_b \) gives the normalised temperature distribution:
</p>

<p>
$$
\frac{\theta(x)}{\theta_b}
=
\cosh(mx)
-
\tanh(mL)\sinh(mx)
$$
</p>

<p>
Using hyperbolic identities, this expression can be rearranged into:
</p>

<p>
$$
\frac{\theta(x)}{\theta_b}
=
\frac{\cosh\big(m(L-x)\big)}{\cosh(mL)}
$$
</p>

<ul>
<li>\(\theta_b\) - temperature difference at the base relative to surroundings</li>
<li>\(\cosh(m(L-x))\) - describes how temperature decays along the rod</li>
<li>\(\cosh(mL)\) - ensures the base temperature boundary condition is satisfied</li>
</ul>

<h3>Worked Example</h3>

<p>
Using the following values:
</p>

<ul>
<li>\(T_{\text{pipe}} = 30 \, \text{K}\)</li>
<li>\(T_{\text{base}} = 300 \, \text{K}\)</li>
<li>\(L = 0.4 \, \text{m}\)</li>
<li>\(r = 0.003 \, \text{m}\)</li>
<li>\(k_{\text{rod}} = 15 \, \text{W/(m·K)}\)</li>
<li>\(h = 5 \, \text{W/(m}^2\text{·K)}\)</li>
</ul>

<h4>Step 1: Define Temperature Difference</h4>

<p>
$$
\theta(x) = T(x) - T_{\text{pipe}}
$$
</p>

<p>
$$
\theta_b = T_{\text{base}} - T_{\text{pipe}} = 300 - 30 = 270 \, \text{K}
$$
</p>

<h4>Step 2: Fin Parameter</h4>

<p>
$$
m = \sqrt{\frac{h P}{k A}}
$$
</p>

<p>
For a cylindrical rod:
</p>

<p>
$$
P = 2\pi r, \qquad A = \pi r^2, \qquad m = \sqrt{\frac{2h}{k r}}
$$
</p>

<p>
Substituting values:
</p>

<p>
$$
m = \sqrt{\frac{2 \cdot 5}{15 \cdot 0.003}} = \sqrt{\frac{10}{0.045}} = \sqrt{222.2} \approx 14.9 \, \text{m}^{-1}
$$
</p>

<h4>Step 3: Temperature Distribution</h4>

<p>
Using the adiabatic tip solution:
</p>

<p>
$$
\frac{\theta(x)}{\theta_b} = \frac{\cosh\big(m(L-x)\big)}{\cosh(mL)}
$$
</p>

<p>
Converting back to temperature:
</p>

<p>
$$
T(x) = \frac{\cosh\big(m(L-x)\big)}{\cosh(mL)} (T_{\text{base}} - T_{\text{pipe}}) + T_{\text{pipe}}
$$
</p>

<!-- Force new page on print -->
<div class="print-page-break"></div>

<h4>Step 4: Evaluate</h4>

<p><strong>At the base (\(x=0\)):</strong></p>

<p>
$$
T(0) = 300 \, \text{K}
$$
</p>

<p><strong>At the tip (\(x=L=0.4\)):</strong></p>

<p>
$$
mL = 14.9 \cdot 0.4 \approx 5.96
$$
</p>

<p>
$$
\cosh(5.96) \approx 193
$$
</p>

<p>
$$
T(L) = \frac{1}{193} \cdot 270 + 30 \approx 31.4 \, \text{K}
$$
</p>
